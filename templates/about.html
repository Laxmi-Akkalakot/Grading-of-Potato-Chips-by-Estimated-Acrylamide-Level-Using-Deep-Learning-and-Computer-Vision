PotatoGuard AI · Academic Research Project

Grading of Potato Chips by Estimated Acrylamide Level Using Deep Learning and Computer Vision

A research-oriented intelligent food-quality analysis system that combines computer vision, image processing and machine learning to estimate acrylamide levels in fried potato chips from their visual characteristics.

Computer Vision Deep Learning Regression Food Quality Research
01 · Introduction

About the Project

PotatoGuard AI is an image-based research application designed to analyze fried potato chip images and estimate their acrylamide concentration. The system uses visual information such as colour, browning and image-derived features to support automated sample assessment. It provides a numerical estimate and maps the result into project-defined categories: Low, Medium, High and Very High.

Project Objective

Develop an image-processing and machine-learning pipeline for research-based estimation of acrylamide levels in fried potato chips.

Visual Analysis

Extract colour, texture and image characteristics that may be associated with frying and browning conditions.

Prediction Output

Present an estimated concentration in µg/kg together with a project-defined grade.

02 · Research Motivation

Problem Statement

Acrylamide can form in potato products during high-temperature frying. Its concentration can vary with potato composition, reducing sugar content, processing temperature, cooking time and other conditions.

Laboratory measurement provides analytical evidence, but requires sample preparation, specialized equipment and trained personnel. This project investigates whether computer vision and machine learning can provide a preliminary image-based screening estimate that supports research and quality-analysis workflows.

Research focus: image-based estimation, not chemical detection
03 · Food Chemistry

Acrylamide Formation in Fried Potatoes

Acrylamide is a process contaminant that can naturally form in carbohydrate-rich foods during high-temperature cooking, including frying, roasting and baking. Potato chips and French fries are examples of foods in which it can occur.

Maillard Reaction and Acrylamide Formation

A major pathway involves the reaction of the amino acid asparagine with reducing sugars during heating.

Free Asparagine Amino acid naturally present in potatoes
Reducing Sugars Glucose and fructose
High-Temperature Heating Frying and other cooking conditions
Acrylamide One product formed through relevant reaction pathways

The Maillard reaction also contributes to the colour, aroma and flavour of cooked foods. Acrylamide formation is influenced by several processing and ingredient factors; visible browning alone does not determine its concentration.

04 · Food Safety

Food Safety Significance

Why Acrylamide Matters

Acrylamide has been studied for its genotoxic and carcinogenic properties. Laboratory animal studies have demonstrated carcinogenic effects at experimental exposure levels. The potential health implications of dietary exposure remain an important subject of scientific and regulatory assessment.

Food-safety research therefore investigates how acrylamide forms, how exposure can be reduced and how levels in foods can be monitored.

IARC Classification
Group 2A

The International Agency for Research on Cancer classifies acrylamide as probably carcinogenic to humans.

This is a hazard classification. It does not mean that every exposure causes cancer or quantify the risk from eating a particular serving of potato chips.

10 · Scientific Validity

Project Limitations

Visual appearance is not chemical measurement

Chip colour and browning may be associated with processing conditions, but they cannot directly reveal the actual acrylamide concentration.

Dataset limitations

Model performance depends on dataset size, diversity, label quality, sample preparation and representative training and testing splits.

Processing variability

Potato variety, sugar content, frying temperature, duration, oil conditions, lighting and camera settings may influence the image or the underlying acrylamide level.

Independent laboratory validation

The regression model must be validated against representative laboratory-measured reference samples before its estimates can be considered reliable for operational food-safety decisions.

Model Explainability

Add explainability techniques, uncertainty estimates and independent performance monitoring.

Laboratory Confirmation Is Essential

PotatoGuard AI provides image-based research estimates only. It does not chemically detect or directly measure acrylamide. Confirmation requires a validated analytical laboratory method, such as gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (GC-MS) or liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS), with suitable sample preparation and quality-control procedures. HPLC-based methods may also be used where validated for the intended matrix and measurement purpose.

Research and Academic Use

This project demonstrates the application of computer vision and machine learning to food-quality research. Its predictions are estimates derived from image appearance and model outputs. They are not laboratory measurements, medical advice, regulatory assessments or a guarantee of food safety. The project's grade thresholds are research-defined categories and are not official regulatory safety limits.
